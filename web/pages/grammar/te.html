Grouping with te

Modifiers group to the right by default. On the rare occasion that gives the wrong reading, te marks where the modifiers end and the word they modify begins.

PikotikaGroups asMeaning
meka aku pestebig [water animal]a big fish
meka aku te peste[big water] animala sea creature

Without te, meka reaches all the way to peste. With it, it can't — the particle is in the way — so it settles on aku instead, and meka aku as a unit modifies the animal.

She's a business owner who is small.

Tisomo ri piko komparmen teneomo.

She's a small-business owner.

Tisomo ri piko komparmen te teneomo.

When you need it

Almost never. Three conditions have to hold at once: three or more words in a row, and the grouping you want is the left one, and no standing compound already says it.

te does nothing between two words — there is only one way to group them, and spacing and stress have already done it. If you find yourself putting te between two words, take it out.

It reaches back to the last particle before it — ri, a, rite, or another te — and everything from there to the particle is the modifier.

PikotikaMeaning
Tis ri verte arpoaku komparroko.It's a tea shop that's green.
Tis ri verte arpoaku te komparroko.It's a green-tea shop.
Tisomo ri verte arpoaku te komparroko te teneomo.She's the owner of a green-tea shop.

Or don't say it that way at all

You can usually get the left grouping by making a compound instead: pikokomparmen ‘small business’, then pikokomparmen teneomo. That is a legitimate option, and sometimes the better one.

But length argues the other way more often than you would think. A compound carries exactly one stress, so a long one leaves a long stretch of syllables with nothing prominent in it — thirteen syllables over two stresses reads as a mouthful, and the same content spaced out reads as speech. Note that this is a reason not to compound; it is never a licence for a te that does no grouping work.

Lexicalized compounds are never broken up

However long a standing compound is, it is a dictionary word, not a phrase you are building as you speak — so no te goes inside it. mekaakurante ‘seashore’ stays one word.

Try it yourself

  1. Which of these means a shop selling green tea? verte arpoaku komparroko or verte arpoaku te komparroko?
    Show answer

    The second. Without te, verte reaches the shop and colors the shop.

  2. Is meka te aku good Pikotika?
    Show answer

    It parses, but the particle is doing nothing: with two words there is only one grouping. Say meka aku.

  3. Tisomo ri arpoaku komparroko te teneomo.
    Show answer

    ‘She's a tea-shop owner.’ te reaches back to ri, so the whole of arpoaku komparroko modifies the owner.
