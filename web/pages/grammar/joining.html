And, or, but, because

Three words join things, and they work at any size — between two words, two phrases, or two whole sentences. None of them changes the grammar of what it joins.

WordSense
kumand
siveor
setsbut
PikotikaMeaning
Rikus sive wo, ker?Cold or warm?
Nino kum eko ri komivori.The child and I are hungry.
Si, sets kampaire ri mas veros.Yes, but walking is faster.
Yantar, eko vemapari ri veni, sets eko viropari ri non veni.Tomorrow my mother is coming, but my father isn't.

sets may open a sentence, picking up from the one before, exactly as English But… does.

But come at seven, and we can eat first.

Sets pam veni in 7 ora, kum ekomen ri kan yer komi.

kum is also the preposition ‘with’ — see Prepositional phrases.

Giving a reason

rason is the noun ‘reason’, and it also opens a clause to mean ‘because’. tisrason (this-reason) is ‘therefore’.

WordHeadsSo the sentence runs
rasonthe causeresult, rason cause
tisrasonthe consequencecause, tisrason result

An explanation can therefore run in either direction, and the word you pick tells the listener which half is coming.

Good, because last time my ears hurt for two days.

Pona, rason in yer tempo eko otiparte ri marasenti in 2 yan turan.

tisrason is also what closes a condition — see Conditions and counterfactuals. And kerrason (what-reason) is ‘why’.

Why do I have to tell them?

Eko ri neses tika ver nontisomo, kerrason?

One thing to watch

A joined clause cannot simply follow a clause that is serving as an object — it would be swallowed into it. Break the sentence and open the next one with the joining word. See Subordinate clauses.

Try it yourself

  1. 20 moni por wun, sive 100 moni por 6.
    Show answer

    ‘Twenty each, or a hundred for six.’ sive joining two phrases, with no sentence in sight.

  2. Tis ri rason.
    Show answer

    ‘That explains it’ — the plain noun, used as the whole description.

  3. Rewrite ‘I'm tired, therefore I'm going home’ as a because sentence.
