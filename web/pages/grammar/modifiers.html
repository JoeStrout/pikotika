Modifiers

Adjective, adverb, and possessive are one operation in Pikotika, not three: put the modifier in front of the word it modifies.

English calls itPikotikaLiterally
an adjectivetuke komisweet food
an adverbveros irefast go
a possessiveeko sanaomoI heal-person

All three are the same thing: something in front, narrowing what follows. The possessor is simply a modifier — there is no my, only eko standing where sweet stood.

We're going to my father's house.

Ekomen ri ire ver eko viropari kase.

Read that from the right: a house, whose father's, whose I. Each modifier scopes over everything after it.

Please speak slowly.

Pam nonveros tika.

The one exception: names

A proper noun comes after its category word, not before — omo Ar, ‘the person Hal’.

I'm going to Lisbon.

Eko ri ire ver sitas Rispan.

See Names and loanwords.
