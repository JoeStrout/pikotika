Questions and answers

Questions rearrange nothing. Put ker ‘what’ in the slot where the answer belongs, and leave the rest of the sentence exactly as it was.

QuestionLiterallyMeaning
Panyu ri kerroko?toilet RI what-placeWhere is the bathroom?
Tu ri ire ver kerroko?you RI go toward what-placeWhere are you going?
Tis ri ker moni?this RI what moneyHow much is this?
Keromo ri sanaomo?what-person RI heal-personWho is the doctor?

The question words

They are all ker plus the root for the kind of thing you are asking about.

WordLiterallyMeaning

Yes-or-no questions

Here there is no slot to fill, so the question word goes at the end after a comma. Which one you choose says how you expect to be answered.

TagYou areExample
, ker?genuinely askingTu ri vite a kanis, ker? Do you see the dog?
, non?expecting yesTu ri vite a kanis, non? You see the dog, don't you?
, si?seeking agreementTu ri vite a kanis, si? You see the dog, right?

Use ker when you do not know the answer. The other two lean, and a listener will feel the lean — so don't use them when you want an honest reply.

Answering

si is the language's one interjection, ‘yes’; non does duty as ‘no’. Either stands alone, or repeat the verb to be unmistakable.

ShortFuller
yesSi.Si, vite.
noNon.Non vite.

Try it yourself

  1. Tu senseyanvin ri ker?
    Show answer

    ‘How was your weekend?’ — literally you seven-sun-finish RI what. English needs how was; Pikotika just asks what it was.

  2. Musi ri apa in kerora?
    Show answer

    ‘What time does the music start?’ kerora is what-hour, sitting in the slot an hour would occupy.

  3. Ask ‘Is the fish fresh?’, genuinely not knowing.
    Show answer
