Can, want, must

Five short "mood" words carry ability, wish, permission and obligation. They are ordinary modifiers, sitting in front of the verb exactly like an adverb.

WordSenseExample
kancan, is ableEko ri kan ire.I can go.
posmight, maybeEko ri pos ire.I might go.
voriwant toEko ri vori ire.I want to go.
posponashouldEko ri pospona ire.I should go.
nesesmust, needs toEko ri neses ire.I must go.

These are ordinary adverbs like veros ‘fast’, in the same slot, doing the same thing — they just happen to make the action something not yet done.

They take no a

A modifier has no object, so none of the five is ever followed by a. The object marker belongs to the verb they sit in front of:

I want to eat meat.

Eko ri vori komi a karne.

But vori and neses are also ordinary verbs — want and need — and an ordinary verb takes an object like any other. That is what an a after one of them tells you:

PikotikaMeaningWhy
Eko ri vori ire.I want to go. modifier — one predicate, and you do the going
Eko ri vori a ire.I want travel. verb — the object is a thing
Eko ri vori a tu ri ire.I want you to go. verb — the object is a whole clause

Rule of thumb: Leave the a out whenever the next word is plainly an action. Put it in when what follows is a thing, or a full clause containing ri.

Add vin for the past

Any of the five with the vin aspect marker indicates regret, a missed possibility, or a conclusion from evidence.

PikotikaMeaning
Eko ri kan ire vin.I could have gone.
Eko ri pos ire vin.I might have gone.
Eko ri vori ire vin.I wanted to go [but didn't].
Eko ri pospona ire vin.I should have gone.
Eko ri neses ire vin.I must have gone.

There is no polite would

English hedges with would for politeness; Pikotika does not have this. Just state desires plainly with vori (want) and kusta (like).

EnglishPikotika
I would like to go.Eko ri vori ire.
I think you would like it.Eko ri pensa a tu ri kusta a tis.
I would rather walk.Eko ri mas kusta a kampaire.
