Time and aspect

Verbs never change form. vite is see, saw, seen and seeing; the work tenses do in other languages is done by ordinary words in Pikotika.

Time is stated once, then assumed

Say when you are talking about, and it holds until you change it. Nothing needs re-marking.

Yesterday I saw the dog.

Yanyer, eko ri vite a kanis.

Tomorrow I will see the dog.

Yantar, eko ri vite a kanis.

The verb is identical in both. The listener/reader would continue to assume the same time period in subsequent sentences, until you specify otherwise.

The last three rows are a pattern, not three words to memorize: yer and tar in front of any unit of time give the whole series — yer senseyan ‘last week’, tar anyo ‘next year’, etc.

tisyan and tisnemyan are marked any because a day is a frame, not a moment — today contains a past, a present, and a future. To place something inside it, add a narrower phrase (in suryan ‘in the morning’, in metseyan ‘at midday’, in tunyan ‘in the evening’) or let an aspect marker do it:

Today I have already eaten.

Tisyan, eko ri komi vin.

This evening I'm eating fish.

In tunyan, eko ri komi a akupeste.

Three aspect markers

Aspect — starting, finished, still going — is one word at the end.

MarkerSenseExample
apabeginningEko ri komi apa. I'm starting to eat.
vinfinishedEko ri komi vin. I have eaten.
sistastill goingEko ri komi sista. I'm still eating.

Combine a marker with a stated time and you get the rest of English's tenses for free: Yanyer, eko ri komi vin. is ‘I had eaten’.

These can be used in any combination:
MarkerIn the pastIn the presentIn the future

sistawas still eatingam still eatingwill still be eating

The aspect marker goes last, after the object

Never put apa, vin, or sista (when used as an aspect marker) before a; place it after the object, if any, or at the end of the sentence instead.

I am still eating.

Eko ri komi sista.
If you see one of these words before a, then you know it's not being used as an aspect marker; it's the main verb.

Please stop the car.

Pam sista a karo.

The aspect marker closes the whole description, not the verb. This is clearest when there is no verb at all:

I am still here.

Eko ri in tisroko sista.

A prepositional phrase may sit on either side of the marker. One that is part of what you are saying goes inside, before it, like the example above; one that merely frames the sentence in time or place may follow it.

The light has been broken for months.

Vortoyan ri rompe vin in mur mese turan.

Try it yourself

  1. Musi ri apa in ots ora.
    Show answer

    ‘The music starts at eight.’ Here apa is the ordinary verb ‘begin’, not an aspect marker.

  2. Eko ri vite a kanis vin.
    Show answer

    ‘I have seen the dog.’ The aspect marker (vin) comes after the object (kanis).

  3. ‘I had already finished.’ (Start with Yertempo to place this sentence in the past.)
    Show answer

    Yertempo, eko ri vin. — state the past time once, and vin does the rest.
