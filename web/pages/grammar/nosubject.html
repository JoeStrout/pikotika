Commands and indefinite subjects

Two kinds of sentence have no subject, and the difference between them is one particle.

PikotikaMeaningKind
Sista!Stop!command
Ri sista!It's stopping!indefinite subject

A command drops ri along with the subject — there is nobody to describe, only an instruction for the listener. An indefinite subject keeps ri: there is a subject, but it's unspecified. Usually, it's a nebulous, ill-defined subject, like the it in "it's raining," or the they in "that's what they say."

Commands

Start with the verb. Add pam ‘please’ in front to make it a request rather than an order.
PikotikaMeaning
Sista!Stop!
Pam komi.Please eat.
Pam tonar a tis ver eko.Please give that to me.
Pam servi!Please help!

Sentences with nobody in particular

English has to put something in the subject slot, so it invents it and there, or rewrites the sentence as a passive. Pikotika just leaves the slot empty and opens with ri.

EnglishPikotika
It's raining.Ri ruva.
There is a problem.Ri marumkosa.
My luggage has been lost.Ri perti a eko irevaso.
Food is sold here.Ri kompar a komi in tisroko.
Is this allowed?Ri reke a tis, ker?
Payment is required.Ri neses tonar a moni.
They say time is money.Ri tika a tempo ri moni.

Note that there is no separate passive sentence construction in Pikotika; just use an indefinite subject, as in the examples above.
