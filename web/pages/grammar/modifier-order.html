Stacking modifiers

Two or more modifiers in a row follow the order you already use in your own language. This page is mostly permission to stop reading.

They group to the right

The most intrinsic property sits nearest the head and combines with it first. In that big red ball: it's a red ball; what kind of red ball, a big one; which big red ball, that one. Pikotika does the same, in the same order.

I want that big red ball.

Eko ri vori a nontis meka rus rotun.

Languages that put adjectives before the noun are strikingly consistent about this order:

quantity → opinion → size → age → shape → color → origin → material → noun

That is why English says big red ball and not red big ball. If your language works this way, do not study the list — just say what feels natural and you will be right.

Degree words attach to the adjective, not the noun

mas ‘more’, nonmas ‘less’, surmesur ‘too’, meka ‘very’ and piko ‘slightly’ modify the word directly after them, which is why they look like an exception and aren't.

She's a happier person.

Tisomo ri mas konten omo.

In a more happy person, more modifies happy, not happy person. Same in Pikotika.

One real ambiguity

meka is both ‘big’ and ‘very’, and piko both ‘small’ and ‘slightly’. So meka rus rotun could in principle be ‘a very red ball’ rather than ‘a big red ball’. In practice context and stress settle it, and if they don't, a compound will — see Coining new words.

Parallel modifiers take kum

Stacking is for modifiers at different levels. Two that describe the head equally are joined with kum ‘and’, exactly as in English — cold and sweet, not cold sweet.

Try it yourself

  1. Eko ri vite a piko akupeste.

    ‘I see a small fish.’ One modifier, no ordering question to answer.

  2. Eko mas anyomeka samparivema

    ‘my older sister’ — I more year-big same-parent-woman. mas binds to anyomeka, and that binds to the sister.

  3. Which is ‘too short’: surmesur piko or piko surmesur?
    Show answer

    Surmesur piko. The degree word comes first, because it modifies the adjective that follows it.
