Conditions and counterfactuals

A condition opens with pos and its consequence opens with tisrason ‘therefore’. Both halves are ordinary sentences; nothing else changes.

pos the condition tisrason what follows from it

If you come, we'll be happy.

Pos tu ri veni, tisrason ekomen ri konten.

If the train is late, please tell me.

Pos metarrinekaro ri tar, tisrason pam tika ver eko.

Do not leave out tisrason. Without it the two clauses are symmetric and a listener cannot tell which one is the condition.

Notice also what is not there. English marks the second clause too, with will or would. tisrason has already done that work, so the verb stays plain.

Counterfactuals: nonves

A counterfactual is just a condition you already know to be false, so Pikotika marks it on the condition rather than on the verb. Swap pos for nonves and change nothing else.

PikotikaMeaning
Pos eko ri tene a moni, tisrason eko ri kompar a tis. If I have the money, I'll buy it. (maybe I do)
Nonves eko ri tene a moni, tisrason eko ri kompar a tis. If I had the money, I would buy it. (I don't)
Nonves eko ri tene a moni vin, tisrason eko ri kompar a tis vin. If I had had the money, I would have bought it. (I didn't)

The two markers are exact opposites: pos leaves the condition open, nonves denies it. As a plain word nonves just means ‘false’, and this is the same word doing the same job — falsely, I have the money. There is nothing extra to learn.

Past time comes from vin, as everywhere else. The consequence needs no counterfactual marking of its own: if the condition is false, so is what follows from it.

Without a stated condition

A bare modal already carries the sense — Eko ri pospona apa yer vin. is ‘I should have started earlier’, and, exactly as in English, saying it implies you didn't. Do not add nonves to these.

Try it yourself

  1. Pos tu ri kansa, tisrason ekomen ri kan retsire.
    Show answer

    ‘If you're tired, we can go back.’

  2. Nonves eko ri sape vin, tisrason eko ri veni yer vin.
    Show answer

    ‘If I had known, I would have come earlier.’ Two vins, both doing plain past work.

  3. Say ‘If it rains, we'll stay home.’
    Show answer

    Pos ri ruva, tisrason ekomen ri sista in kase. — the condition has no subject, so it opens with bare ri.
