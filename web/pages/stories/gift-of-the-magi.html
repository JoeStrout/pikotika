Ponapensaomo tonarkosa

The Gift of the Magi

O. Henry's Christmas story of 1905, retold in plain Pikotika. Tap any underlined word for its meaning. Under each paragraph, English opens a plain translation of the Pikotika — not O. Henry's own words, which say a good deal more. His original is free to read at Project Gutenberg.

1 moni kum 87 inkatonmoni. Tis ri pan. Kum 60 inkatonmoni ri piko metarmoni. Vema Tera ri tene a tis piko tar piko. Tisomo ri patar kum komparomo topi pan monimesur. Tis ri ekomarumsenti a tisomo. 3 tempo, Tera ri mesur a tis. 1.87 moni. Kum yantar ri Kurismas.

English

One dollar and 87 cents. That was all. And 60 cents of it was small coins. Della had kept it little by little. She fought with the shopkeepers over every price, and it shamed her. Three times Della counted it. 1.87 dollars. And tomorrow was Christmas.

Ri nem ar kosa por vake. Tera ri seta sur vetus ronkasetauter, kum tisomo ri meka viteaku. Viva ri mur viteaku kum piko kontenpuka.

English

There was nothing else to do. Della sat on the old couch and cried hard. Life is a lot of crying and a little smiling.

Tera ri viteaku sista. Pam vite a tisomo kase. Ri turankompar a tis por 8 moni in senseyan. Tis ri piko kum vetus.

English

Della is still crying. Look at her home. It is rented for 8 dollars a week. It is small and old.

Tun, in porta, ri kitakarta vaso kum vorto oti uter. Sets tis ri rompe. Kum ri nomenkarta: Yemus Tirinam Yan.

English

Downstairs, at the door, there was a letter box and an electric bell. But they were broken. And there was a name card: James Dillingham Young.

Tis ri Tera marisomo nomen. Yertempo, tisomo ri monitoma a 30 moni in senseyan. Tistempo, tisomo ri monitoma a son 20. Sets in kase, tisomo nomen ri Yim, kum Tera ri manutene a tisomo.

English

That is the name of Della's husband. He used to earn 30 dollars a week. Now he earns only 20. But at home his name is Jim, and Della hugs him.

Tera ri viteaku vin. Tisomo ri sista nir vitevuko. Tisomo ri vite a rapiskoror kasepeste in rapiskoror roko. Yantar ri Kurismas, kum Tera ri tene a son 1.87 moni por kompar a tonarkosa por Yim. In mur mese turan, tisomo ri tene a pan inkatonmoni. Tis ri pomo. 20 moni in senseyan ri non mur. Monimesur ri mas meka vons tisomo pensa. Pantempo, tis ri sam. In mur ora turan, Tera ri konten pensa a pona tonarkosa por Yim. Tisomo ri vori a ri nir sam pona vons Yim rite kosa.

English

Della finished crying. She stood near the window. She looked at a gray cat in a gray place. Tomorrow was Christmas, and Della had only 1.87 dollars to buy a gift for Jim. For many months she had kept every cent. This was the result. Twenty dollars a week is not much. Prices were bigger than she thought. It is always so. For many hours Della had happily thought about a good gift for Jim. She wanted something almost as good as Jim.

Mets vitevuko, ri ronka kum piko ekoviteuter. Tera ri piko; tisrason tisomo ri kan vite a tisomo in tis.

English

Between the windows there was a long, narrow mirror. Della was slim, so she could see herself in it.

Tera ri veros kurva ver ekoviteuter. Tisomo viteparte ri yan. Sets in 20 pikoparte, tisomo ri tiven anka. Tisomo ri veros tun a tisomo testarine. Tis ri meka ronka.

English

Della turned quickly to the mirror. Her eyes were bright. But in 20 seconds she became pale. Quickly she let down her hair. It was very long.

Yim kum Tera ri meka ekoponasenti topi pits kosa. Wun ri Yim yorometar orauter. Yertempo, tisomo viropari kum paripari ri tene a tis. Ar ri Tera testarine. Tis pits kosa ri mas pesaneses vons pan mekamoni kosa.

English

Jim and Della were very proud of two things. One was Jim's gold watch. Before, his father and grandfather had owned it. The other was Della's hair. These two things mattered more than any costly thing.

Tera testarine ri tun, sam vons terakoror ronkaaku. Tis ri ronka ver tisomo kampa. Tis ri nir ropa por tisomo. Tar, tisomo ri pikotimo. Tisomo ri veros repe sur a tis. Wun tempo, tisomo ri sista, kum wun sive pits viteaku ri tun ver vetus rus teraropa.

English

Della's hair fell like a brown river. It was long, down to her legs. It was almost clothing for her. Then she grew nervous. Quickly she put it up again. Once she stopped, and a tear or two fell on the old red carpet.

Tisomo ri ropatene a vetus terakoror ropa kum testaropa. Tisomo viteparte ri yan sista. Tisomo ri veros eksire vons kase. Tisomo ri tun ire in surmoto ver moto.

English

She put on her old brown coat and hat. Her eyes were still bright. She hurried out of the house. She went down the stairs to the street.

Tisomo ri sista in komparroko. Nomen ri tika a: “Vema Sovuroni. Pan testarine kosa.”

Tera ri veros surire. Tisomo ri sista; tisomo ri kansa. Vema Sovuroni ri meka, surmesur anka, kum rikus.

English

She stopped at a shop. The sign said: "Madame Sofronie. All things for hair." Della hurried up the stairs. She stopped; she was out of breath. Madame Sofronie was large, too pale, and cold.

Tera ri kertika: “Tu ri kompar a eko testarine, ker?”

Vema Sovuroni ri tika: “Eko ri kompar a testarine. Pam eks a tu testaropa. Eko ri vori vite a tu testarine.”

Tera testarine ri repe tun. Vema Sovuroni ri sur a testarine. Tisomo ri tika: “20 moni.”

Tera ri tika: “Pam veros tonar a tis ver eko.”

Tar, in 2 ora turan, Tera ri tentarvite in pan komparroko por Yim tonarkosa. Tisomo ri konten.

English

Della asked, "Will you buy my hair?" Madame Sofronie said, "I buy hair. Take off your hat. I want to see your hair." Della's hair came down again. Madame lifted the hair. She said, "20 dollars." Della said, "Give it to me quickly." Then for two hours Della searched every shop for Jim's gift. She was happy.

Invin, tisomo ri vite a tis. Ri vake a tis por Yim, kum non por ar omo. Ri nem sam kosa in ar komparroko. Tis ri orauter rine vons pona metar, kum ri nem ar kosa sur tis. Tis ri sam pona vons orauter. Tisomo ri vite a tis in kertempo, tisomo ri sape a tis ri por Yim. Tis ri sam vons Yim: pikooti kum pona. Tisomo ri tonar a 21 moni por tis. Tisomo ri tene a 87 inkatonmoni sista. Tisomo ri veros retsire ver kase. Tisrason, Yim ri kan vite a ora in pan roko. Orauter ri meka pona, sets tis ri tene a son vetus rine. Tisrason, Yim ri vite a tis in nontika moto.

English

At last she found it. It was made for Jim and for no one else. There was nothing like it in the other shops. It was a watch chain of fine metal, with nothing else on it. It was as good as the watch. When she saw it, she knew it was for Jim. It was like Jim: quiet and good. She paid 21 dollars for it. She still had 87 cents. She hurried home. Now Jim could check the time anywhere. The watch was very fine, but it had only an old cord. So Jim looked at it secretly.

In kase, Tera ri tiven pikokonten. Tisomo ri wo a testarine kurva uter. Tisomo ri sana a tisomo testarine apa. Tis ri meka opus.

English

At home, Della calmed down. She heated the curling iron. She began to fix her hair. That is a big job.

In 40 parte turan, tisomo testa ri tene a mur piko kurva. Tisomo ri samvite a viro nino. Tisomo ri vite a tisomo in ekoviteuter in ronka turan.

English

In 40 minutes her head had many small curls. She looked like a boy. She looked at herself in the mirror for a long time.

Tera ri tika: “Yim ri pos nonviva a eko; sive tisomo ri tika a eko ri samvite a musiire vema. Sets eko ri kan vake a ker? Eko ri tene a son 1.87 moni!”

English

Della said, "Jim might kill me; or else he'll say I look like a dancing girl. But what could I do? I had only 1.87 dollars!"

In 7 ora, neropipe ri yervin, kum karne ri yervin por komivake.

English

At 7 o'clock the coffee was ready, and the meat was ready to cook.

Yim ri nemtempo tar. Tera ri tene a orauter rine in manu. Tisomo ri seta sur tapur nir porta. Tar, tisomo ri oti a Yim ri veni in surmoto. Tisomo ri tiven anka in piko turan. Tisomo ri pikooti tika: “Panvakeomo, pam vake a Yim ri pensa a eko ri ponavite sista.”

English

Jim was never late. Della held the watch chain in her hand. She sat on the table near the door. Then she heard Jim coming on the stairs. For a moment she turned pale. She said quietly, "God, please make Jim think I am still pretty."

Porta ri apa. Yim ri veni, kum tisomo ri vin a porta. Tisomo ri piko, kum tisomo ri non kontenpuka. Tisomo anyoturan ri son 22, sets tisomo ri tene a parimen. Tisomo ri neses a nova rikus ropa, kum tisomo ri tene a nem manu ropa.

English

The door opened. Jim came in, and he closed the door. He was thin, and he was not smiling. He was only 22, but he had a family. He needed a new winter coat, and he had no gloves.

Yim ri sista in porta. Tisomo ri non ire. Tisomo ri son vite a Tera. Tera ri non kan vite a tisomo sentipitur, kum tis ri timo a Tera. Tis ri non raye sive sorpe sive timo. Tera ri non pensa a tis vin.

English

Jim stopped in the doorway. He did not move. He only looked at Della. Della could not understand his expression, and it frightened her. It was not anger or surprise or fear. Della had not expected it.

Tera ri tun ire vons tapur. Tisomo ri ire ver Yim. “Yim, eko mekakusta, pam non vite a eko in tis moto. Eko ri rompe a eko testarine, kum eko ri kompar a tis. Eko ri neses tonar a tonarkosa ver tu in Kurismas! Testarine ri repe tiven ronka. Tu ri non raye topi tis, si? Eko ri neses vake a tis. Eko testarine ri meka veros tiven ronka. Pam tika a pona Kurismas, Yim. Ekomen ri pospona konten. Tu ri non sape a eko ri tene a meka ponavite tonarkosa por tu!”

English

Della got down from the table. She went to Jim. "Jim, my love, please don't look at me this way. I cut my hair, and I sold it. I had to give you a gift at Christmas! The hair will grow long again. You won't be angry about it, right? I had to do it. My hair grows very fast. Please say 'Merry Christmas,' Jim. We should be happy. You don't know what a beautiful gift I have for you!"

Yim ri nonveros kertika: “Tu ri rompe a tu testarine vin, ker?”

Tera ri tika: “Eko ri rompe a tis, kum eko ri kompar a tis. Tu ri kusta a eko sista, non? Eko ri eko sista, non?”

Yim ri vite a pan kamar. Yim ri tika: “Tu ri tika a tu testarine ri perti vin, ker?”

Tera ri tika: “Pam non tentarvite a tis. Ri kompar a tis vin. Yantar ri Kurismas. Pam ponasenti ver eko, rason eko ri vake a tis por tu. Ri pos kan mesur a eko testarine; sets nem omo ri kan mesur a eko mekakusta por tu. Yim, eko ri pospona komivake a karne, ker?”

English

Jim asked slowly, "You've cut your hair?" Della said, "I cut it, and I sold it. You still like me, don't you? I'm still me, aren't I?" Jim looked around the whole room. Jim said, "You say your hair is gone?" Della said, "Please don't look for it. It's been sold. Tomorrow is Christmas. Please be kind to me, because I did this for you. Maybe someone could count my hairs; but no one can count my love for you. Jim, shall I cook the meat?"

Tar, Yim ri veros ire. Tisomo ri manutene a Tera. Pam vite a ar kosa in 10 pikoparte turan. 8 moni in senseyan, sive 1000000 moni in anyo: tis ri ar, ker? Ponapensaomo ri teneveni a mekamoni tonarkosa, sets non tis. Tartempo, tu ri vite a tis.

English

Then Jim moved quickly. He hugged Della. Please look at something else for 10 seconds. Eight dollars a week, or a million a year: is there a difference? The wise men brought expensive gifts, but not this. Later you will understand.

Yim ri toma a piko vaso vons tisomo ropa. Tisomo ri seta a tis sur tapur. Yim ri tika: “Ter, pam non marum vite a eko. Nem testarine rompe ri kan vake a eko ri nonmas kusta a tu. Sets pos tu ri apa a nontis vaso, tisrason tu ri vite a rason.”

English

Jim took a small box out of his coat. He put it on the table. Jim said, "Dell, please don't misunderstand me. No haircut could make me like you less. But if you open that box, you will see the reason."

Tera ri veros apa a vaso. Tisomo ri meka konten. Tar, veros, tisomo ri meka viteaku. Yim ri neses manutene a tisomo. In vaso ri testarine uter. Yertempo, Tera ri kusta vite a tis in komparroko vitevuko in ronka turan. Tis ri meka ponavite, kum meka mekamoni. Tera ri meka vori a tis, sets tisomo ri sape a tisomo ri non kan kompar a tis. Tistempo, Tera ri tene a tis; sets tisomo ri tene a nem testarine por tis. Sets Tera ri manutene a tis. Tisomo ri viteaku sista, sets tisomo ri kontenpuka. Tisomo ri tika: “Eko testarine ri meka veros tiven ronka, Yim!”

English

Della quickly opened the box. She was very happy. Then, quickly, she cried hard. Jim had to hug her. In the box were combs. For a long time Della had liked looking at them in a shop window. They were very beautiful, and very expensive. Della wanted them badly, but she knew she could not buy them. Now Della had them; but she had no hair for them. But Della hugged them. She was still crying, but she smiled. She said, "My hair grows very fast, Jim!"

Tar, Tera ri kampasurire sam vons piko kasepeste. Tisomo ri tika: “O, o!”

Yim ri non vite a tisomo ponavite tonarkosa vin. Tera ri tene a orauter rine sur manu, por Yim. Metar ri yan, sam vons Tera konten. “Tis ri meka pona, non, Yim? Eko ri tentarvite a tis in pan sitas. Tistempo, tu ri pantempo vite a ora! Pam tonar a tu orauter ver eko. Eko ri vori vite a rine sur orauter.”

English

Then Della jumped like a little cat. She said, "Oh, oh!" Jim had not seen his beautiful gift yet. Della held the watch chain on her hand, for Jim. The metal was bright, like Della's happiness. "It's very good, isn't it, Jim? I searched the whole city for it. Now you'll look at the time all the time! Give me your watch. I want to see the chain on the watch."

Sets Yim ri non tonar a orauter. Tisomo ri seta sur ronkasetauter. Tisomo ri kontenpuka. “Ter, ekomen ri pospona seta a ekomen Kurismas tonarkosa in vaso in piko turan. Tis ri surmesur pona por tistempo. Eko ri kompar a orauter; tisrason eko ri tene a moni por tu testarine uter. Kum tistempo, pam komivake a karne.”

English

But Jim did not give her the watch. He sat on the couch. He smiled. "Dell, we should put our Christmas gifts in a box for a while. They're too good for now. I sold the watch; that's how I had money for your combs. And now, please cook the meat."

Ponapensaomo ri teneveni a tonarkosa ver nino Yisas. Tisomo ri apa a Kurismas tonarkosa. Tisomo ri ponapensa; tisrason tisomo tonarkosa ri ponapensa kosa. Tis tikakosa ri topi pits marumpensa nino in piko kase. Yim ri tonar a tisomo orauter por Tera. Tera ri tonar a tisomo testarine por Yim. Sets mets ri tonar a tonarkosa rite pan omo, tis pits ri mas ponapensa vons pan. Pantempo kum panroko, tisomo ri mas ponapensa. Tisomo ri ponapensaomo.

English

The wise men brought gifts to the child Jesus. They began Christmas gifts. They were wise, so their gifts were wise things. This story is about two foolish children in a small home. Jim gave his watch for Della. Della gave her hair for Jim. But among all people who give gifts, these two are the wisest. Always and everywhere, they are the wisest. They are the wise ones.
