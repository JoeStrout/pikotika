Grammar

Pikotika grammar is small enough to read in an afternoon. Word order is rigid, no word ever changes form, and there is nothing irregular to memorize — so most of what follows is telling you which of English's rules you can put down.

The short version

Six rules, and nearly everything else is a consequence of one of them.

  1. Word order never changes. Subject ri verb a object — and nothing changes this: not a question, not for emphasis, not inside a subordinate clause. → Basic sentence structure
  2. No word ever changes form. No conjugation, no plurals, no gender, no case, no articles, no agreement. What you learn is what you say. → Nouns and pronouns
  3. Everything after ri describes the subject — and it need not contain a verb. An adjective, a prepositional phrase, or a noun does the job just as well, which is why we need no word for ‘is’. → Describing the subject
  4. Modifiers come before what they modify. Adjective, adverb, and possessive are one operation rather than three — and so is comparison, and so are can, want, and must. → Modifiers
  5. What other languages put in verb endings, Pikotika puts in ordinary words. Time, aspect, ability, obligation, and condition are all vocabulary you already have. → Time and aspect
  6. An often-used phrase can become a word. Close up the spaces, keep one stress, and you have a compound. This is how the language grows without growing. → Coining new words
