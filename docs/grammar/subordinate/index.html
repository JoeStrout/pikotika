Subordinate clauses

A whole sentence can be the object of a verb. Put it after a, and change nothing about it.

I want you to come.

Eko ri vori a tu ri veni.

I think this week will be easy.

Eko ri pensa a tis senseyan ri nonsorin.

No extra marking is needed, because only one ri is allowed per clause. A second one after a can therefore only belong to an embedded clause, and there is nothing else it could mean.

Clauses chain

Each one binds to the verb directly on its left.

PikotikaMeaning
Eko ri vori a tentar a komi a karne.I want to try eating meat.
Eko ri kusta a kitavite a piturmurkarta.I like to read comic books.

A clause runs to the end

It stops at the next a or at the end of the sentence — so anything else has to come before a, not after.

I ask the cook to make it not spicy.

Eko ri voritika ver komivakeomo a tis ri tiven non wosapor.

The phrase naming the cook sits ahead of the clause, because once the clause opens there is no way back out of it.

The consequence: break the sentence to join on

For the same reason, a clause you mean to add with kum, sive or sets cannot follow after a comma — it would be swallowed into the object clause. End the sentence and open the next one with the joining word.

I hope I can find it, or else I'll have to buy a new one.

Eko ri vori a eko ri kan repetoma a tis; sive eko ri neses kompar a nova kosa.

Written with a comma, that sive clause would be part of what is hoped for — ‘I hope that I can find it, or that I must buy a new one’ — which is not the meaning at all.

Try it yourself

  1. Ri tika a tempo ri moni.
    Show answer

    ‘They say time is money.’ No subject on the outer clause, one on the inner.

  2. Tu ri sape a rinekaro ri eksire in kerora, ker?
    Show answer

    ‘Do you know when the train leaves?’ The question word sits inside the embedded clause, in the slot the answer would fill — nothing moves.

  3. Why can't ‘I want you to come, and I want him to eat’ be one sentence with a comma?
    Show answer

    Because the kum half would be read as part of what you want. Break it: Eko ri vori a tu ri veni; kum eko ri vori a tisomo ri komi.
