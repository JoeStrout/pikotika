Nouns and pronouns

A Pikotika noun never changes shape. There is nothing to learn here except which rules you can stop applying.

Four things that don't exist

English hasPikotika has
articles — a, thenothing. nino is ‘child’, ‘a child’, or ‘the child’.
plurals — child / childrennothing. nino is one child or many.
gender — he / she / itnothing. tisomo is any of these.
case — I / menothing. eko is both.

Pronouns

There are four, and they double as the demonstratives.

WordSense
ekoI, me, my
tuyou, your
tisthis, it, the one here
nontisthat, the other one

For a person rather than a thing, add omo: tisomo is ‘he, she, they — this person’, and nontisomo is the other one, or ‘they’ in the sense of people generally.

She's my sister.

Tisomo ri eko samparivema.

‘We’ is a compound, not a form of ‘I’

men is an ordinary root meaning group, so ekomen is literally I-group. It's just a regular compound word.

We're leaving.

Ekomen ri eksire.

The same root does the work of any plural you actually need to state: avismen is a flock, parimen your relatives.

When you do need to say ‘more than one’

Say it with a word, the way you would say ‘several’ or ‘all’ in English. Number, quantity, and mur or pan all sit in front of the noun like any other modifier.

PikotikaMeaning
mur kartamany pages
pan omoall people
4 rotunpomofour apples

Here, now, today

tis combines with a handful of roots to point at the current one of anything:

WordLiterallyMeaning
tisrokothis-placehere
tistempothis-timenow
tisyanthis-suntoday

I'm here.

Eko ri in tisroko.

Try it yourself

  1. Panomo ri tika a tis.
    Show answer

    ‘Everybody says that.’ every-person — a compound, not a special pronoun.

  2. Tu nino ri kermoto?
    Show answer

    ‘How are your children?’ Or, perhaps, ‘How's your child?’ tu nino is your child or your children; context would make it clear.

  3. Ekomen ri tene a kamar.
    Show answer

    ‘We have room’ or ‘We have a room,’ again depending on context.
