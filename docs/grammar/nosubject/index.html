Commands and indefinite subjects

Two kinds of sentence have no subject, and the difference between them is one particle.

commandindefinite subject
Sista! Ri sista!
Stop! It's stopping!

A command drops ri along with the subject — there is nobody to describe, only somebody to instruct. An indefinite subject keeps it: there is a subject, you are simply not saying who.

Commands

Start with the verb. Add pam ‘please’ in front to make it a request rather than an order, which in practice is most of the time.

PikotikaMeaning
Sista!Stop!
Pam komi.Please eat.
Pam tonar a tis ver eko.Please give that to me.
Pam servi!Help, please!

Sentences with nobody in particular

English has to put something in the subject slot, so it invents it and there, or rewrites the sentence as a passive. Pikotika just leaves the slot empty and opens with ri.

EnglishPikotika
It's raining.Ri ruva.
There is a problem.Ri marumkosa.
My luggage has been lost.Ri perti a eko irevaso.
Food is sold here.Ri kompar a komi in tisroko.
Is this allowed?Ri reke a tis, ker?
Payment is required.Ri neses tonar a moni.
They say the doctor is coming.Ri tika a sanaomo ri veni.

Note what the passive costs here: nothing. There is no passive construction to learn, because the only reason English has one is to get the object into the subject slot — and Pikotika is content to leave that slot empty.

Try it yourself

  1. Ri ruva. against Ruva!
    Show answer

    ‘It's raining’ against ‘Rain!’ — an observation against an instruction to the sky.

  2. Ri nem wo aku.
    Show answer

    ‘There's no hot water.’

  3. Ask a waiter to bring water, politely.
    Show answer

    Pam teneveni a aku. — literally please have-come A water.
