Can, want, must

Five short words carry ability, wish, permission and obligation. They are ordinary modifiers, sitting in front of the verb exactly like an adverb.

WordSenseExample
kancan, is ableEko ri kan ire.I can go.
posmight, maybeEko ri pos ire.I might go.
voriwant toEko ri vori ire.I want to go.
posponashouldEko ri pospona ire.I should go.
nesesmust, needs toEko ri neses ire.I must go.

Nothing about them is special grammar. They are words like veros ‘fast’, in the same slot, doing the same thing — they just happen to make the action something not yet done.

Add vin for the past

Any of the five with the vin aspect marker indicates regret, a missed possibility, or a conclusion from evidence.

PikotikaMeaning
Eko ri kan ire vin.I could have gone.
Eko ri pos ire vin.I might have gone.
Eko ri pospona ire vin.I should have gone.
Eko ri neses ire vin.I must have gone.

There is no polite would

English hedges with would for politeness; Pikotika does not have this. Just state desires plainly with vori (want) and kusta (like).

EnglishPikotika
I would like to go.Eko ri vori ire.
I think you would like it.Eko ri pensa a tu ri kusta a tis.
I would rather walk.Eko ri mas kusta a kampaire.

Try it yourself

See if you can translate the following.

  1. Eko ri kan toma a aku, ker?
    Show answer

    ‘May I have some water?’ Permission (may I) and ability (can I) are the same word in Pikotika.

  2. Eko ri neses tormi a eko vortotika.
    Show answer

    ‘I need to charge my phone.’ (Tormi can also mean "recharge.")

  3. Tu ri pos ves.
    Show answer

    ‘You may be right.’

  4. ‘We could have eaten together.’
    Show answer
