Meeting people

A first meeting asks for four things: a hello, a name, where you are from, and a reason to keep talking. The root under all of it is sarve meet.

Hello

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Novasarve!new-meet Hello! (meeting for the first time)
Kontensarve!happy-meet Hello! (anyone, any time)
Eko ri konten sarve a tu.I RI happy meet A you I'm happy to meet you.
Kontenveni!happy-come Welcome!
Sammoto.same-manner Likewise.

Pona suryan and the rest of the time-of-day greetings, and every goodbye, are on Pleasantries and filler.

Names

Names are capitalized, as in English. Usually the name alone is all you need; but when you need to be clear that you're giving the name of a person, you can precede it with omo.

When naming something that's not a person, you might want to preced it with roko (place), sitas (city), nasyon (country), komparroko (shop), or komparmen (company).

PikotikaMeaning
Tu nomen ri ker?What's your name?
Eko nomen ri Aras.My name is Alice.
Eko ri Aras.I'm Alice.
Tisomo ri omo Popu.This is Bob.
Tisomo ri eko ami Popu.This is my friend Bob.
Pam sarve a eko ami Popu.Meet my friend Bob.
Perton — tu nomen ri ker repe?Sorry — what was your name again?
Tis ri eko nomenkarta.Here's my card.

Your own name gets adapted to the fifteen letters Pikotika has, and it keeps its Latin spelling even in Han text — Alice is Aras, Bob is Popu. See Names and loanwords for yours.

Where you're from

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Tu ri vons kerroko?you RI from what-place Where are you from?
Eko ri vons Kanata.I RI from Canada I'm from Canada.
Eko ri Kanata omo.I RI Canada person I'm Canadian.
Eko ri viva in Itaria, sets eko ri vons Kanata.I RI life in Italia, but I RI from Canada I live in Italy, but I'm from Canada.
Tu ri in tisroko yer vin, ker?you RI in this-place before finish, what Have you been here before?
Tu ri in tisroko por opus sive por yu, ker?you RI in this-place for work or for play, what Are you here for work or for pleasure?

Notice the two uses of a country name: vons Kanata names the place you came from, while Kanata omo is an ordinary modifier — a Canada person. There is no separate word for ‘Canadian’.

Languages

PikotikaMeaning
Tu ri tika a Inkuris, ker?Do you speak English?
Tu tikamoto ri ker?What language do you speak?
Eko ri tika a piko Pikotika.I speak a little Pikotika.
Eko ri impar a Pikotika sista.I'm still learning Pikotika.
Pam nonveros tika.Please speak slowly.
Tonkuo omo ri tika a Tonwen.A Chinese person speaks Chinese.

A language's name is whatever its own speakers call it, approximated like any other loanword — Vuranse comes from français, not from English French. The country name works the same way; Tonkuo is China, Tonwen is Chinese. See Names and loanwords.

How you know each other

PikotikaMeaning
Tu ri sape a Karra in kermoto?How do you know Carla?
Ekomen ri yunta opus.We work together.
Ekomen ri sarve in imparroko.We met at school.
Tisomo ri eko ami.She's a friend of mine.
Eko ri veni kum eko ami.I came with a friend.
Eko ri non pensa a ekomen ri sarve vin.I don't think we've met.
Ekomen ri sarve vin, non?We've met before, haven't we?

in kermoto asks by what route, not what state — the answer is the story of how it happened. See Prepositional phrases.

Once you are past this, everything else is Small talk.

A whole conversation

Carla introduces two of her friends at a party.

PikotikaEnglish
Karra: Aras, pam sarve a eko ami Popu.Carla: Alice, meet my friend Bob.
Aras: Novasarve, Popu. Eko nomen ri Aras.Alice: Hello, Bob. My name is Alice.
Popu: Eko ri konten sarve a tu. Perton — tu nomen ri ker repe?Bob: Happy to meet you. Sorry — what was your name again?
Aras: Aras. Tis ri Kanata nomen.Alice: Alice. It's a Canadian name.
Popu: Tu ri vons Kanata, ker?Bob: You're from Canada?
Aras: Si, sets eko ri viva in Itaria in tistempo.Alice: Yes, but I live in Italy now.
Popu: Sorpe! Tu ri tika a Itariano, ker?Bob: Wow! Do you speak Italian?
Aras: Piko. Eko ri impar sista.Alice: A little. I'm still learning.
Popu: Kum tu ri sape a Karra in kermoto?Bob: And how do you know Carla?
Aras: Ekomen ri yunta opus in monikase. Kum tu, ker?Alice: We work together at a bank. And you?
Popu: Ekomen ri sarve in imparroko, mur anyo yer.Bob: We met at school, many years ago.
Popu: Eko ri konten sarve a tu, Aras.Bob: It was good to meet you, Alice.
Aras: Sammoto!Alice: Likewise!
