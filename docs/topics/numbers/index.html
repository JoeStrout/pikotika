Numbers

Pikotika writes numbers in the digits you already use, so there is nothing to learn about writing them. All you learn is how to say them — ten digit words, four words for the powers of ten, and one pattern with no exceptions in it.

Try

The ten digits

Every digit from two to nine ends in a -ts, -ks, or -ns, so a run of them is easy to hear apart from the words around it.

  • 0 nem 无
  • 1 wun 一
  • 2 pits
  • 3 tets
  • 4 wats
  • 5 kins
  • 6 siks
  • 7 sens
  • 8 ots
  • 9 noks

And four more words

  • 10 tekas 十
  • 100 katon 百
  • 1000 kiru 千
  • 1000000 miron 兆

That is the whole vocabulary. Fourteen words say every number there is.

Japanese and Korean readers: 兆 here is a million, 106, not 1012. Pikotika groups by thousands, the way the metric prefixes do, so 兆 lines up with mega-.

Putting them together

Count the hundreds, then say katon. Count the tens, then say tekas.There are no special words for eleven through nineteen, nor for twenty, thirty, forty: twenty is simply two tens.

NumberSaidLiterally
11tekas wun ten one
20pits tekas two ten
35tets tekas kins three ten five
100katon hundred
500kins katon five hundred
678siks katon sens tekas ots six hundred seven ten eight

Notice 100 and 11: you leave the wun off in front of a power of ten. One hundred is just katon; saying wun katon is not wrong, but it's longer than it needs to be.

Past a thousand nothing changes except that the groups get a comma, which is a pause when you say it aloud:

12345

tekas pits kiru, tets katon wats tekas kins

Write no thousands separators. Half the world puts a comma where the other half puts a period, so Pikotika writes 12345 and leaves the gap out. The period is always the decimal point.

Parts of a number

Four small extensions, each one word.

WrittenSaidWhat the extra word is
1.25wun parte pits kins parte part is the decimal point; the digits after it are read one at a time
3/4tets in wats in is the same everyday word as English in — three in four
50%kins tekas inkaton inkaton in-hundred is a percent: the fraction whose denominator everyone already knows
kinsorten kinsorten orten sequence on the end makes an ordinal — fifth. The one number written in letters, because it is a single compound word

A fraction is written with a slash, just as in English: 3/4. To read out loud, just read the slash as in. Note that in is doing nothing special here — it is the ordinary preposition, and three in four is what 3/4 means.

Ordinals are the one number written in letters, because each is a single compound word. Every one you are likely to want is a standing word — wunorten, pitsorten, tetsorten and so on up through tekasorten, plus katonorten, kiruorten and mironorten — and they keep working inside a larger number, since only the last word takes the suffix: 21st is pits tekas wunorten.

Counting things

A number is an ordinary modifier, so it goes straight in front of what it counts. Nothing agrees with it, and there is no plural to remember.

Two nights.

2 nemyan.

One room, one bed.

1 kamar, 1 tormi.

You may write the number as a digit or as its word — 2 nemyan and pits nemyan are the same two nights, said the same way. Digits are the usual choice, and the only choice once the number is long.

For an amount you are not counting exactly, three ordinary words do the work: nem no, mur many, and pan all. To ask, use ker mesur — what amount — or, for a price, ker moni.

How much are the bananas?

Ronkayoropomo ri ker moni?

Twenty each, or a hundred for six.

20 moni por wun, sive 100 moni por 6.

Six bananas and four apples — one hundred sixty.

6 ronkayoropomo kum 4 rotunpomo: 160 moni.

Numbers in the wild

There's a 30% chance of rain.

Ri 30 inkaton ruva pos.

How old are you?

Tu anyoturan ri ker?

I'm 34 years old.

Eko anyoturan ri 34.

Room twelve, up the stairs, on the right.

Kamar 12, sur in surmoto, in rem.

The numbers are all there.

Pan mesurtika ri in kitakarta.

Clock times, dates, and years all reuse exactly these words with one more word standing in for the punctuation — ora for the colon, and anyo, mese and yan for a year, a month and a day. Those get their own pages, under Telling time and Dates and weekdays.
