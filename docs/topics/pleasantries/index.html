Pleasantries and filler

Courtesy in Pikotika runs on three short roots — pam, mersi and perton — plus pona in front of whatever you are wishing someone. There are no polite verb forms and no honorifics: you are polite by saying the courteous word, not by changing the grammar.

PikotikaLiterallyWhat it does
pamplease turns an order into a request
mersithank thanks, gratitude
pertonsorry sorry, excuse me, pardon
ponagood opens a good wish

Please

pam goes in front of the verb, where an ordinary command would start.

PikotikaMeaning
Pam veni.Please come.
Pam servi!Help, please!
Pam repe tika.Please say that again.
Pam nonveros tika.Please speak slowly.

It can also stand alone at the end, after a comma — an afterthought tacked onto something that was not a full sentence.

PikotikaMeaning
Rikus, pam.Cold, please.
Kum 2 akuvaso, pam.And two glasses of water, please.
Si, pam.Yes, please.
Si, pam tonar.Yes, please do. (hand it over)

pam softens a request, not a statement. Pikotika has no polite would: say what you want plainly with vori, and nobody hears it as brusque. See Can, want, must.

Thanks, and answering it

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Mersi.thank Thanks.
Non, mersi.no, thankNo, thank you.
Meka mersi!big thank Thank you very much!
Eko ri mersi a tu.I RI thank A you I thank you. (the full sentence)
Nem marumkosa.no bad-thing No problem. / You're welcome.
Nem kosa.no thing Never mind. / It doesn't matter.

Nem marumkosa answers thanks and an apology both — it says there was nothing wrong to begin with.

Sorry and excuse me

One word covers the whole range, from bumping someone's elbow to a real apology. Said alone it is light; put it in a sentence for deeper feeling.

PikotikaMeaning
Perton.Sorry. / Oops. / Excuse me.
Perton — tu ri tika a Inkuris, ker?Excuse me — do you speak English?
Eko ri perton.I'm sorry.
Eko ri sape; eko ri perton.I know, I'm sorry.
Eko ri non vori vake a marumkosa.I don't want to cause any trouble.

The same root gives both goodbyes: pertonire sorry-go and pertoneksire sorry-out-go — you are sorry to be leaving.

Hello

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Kontensarve!happy-meet Hello!
Novasarve!new-meet Hello! (when meeting for the first time)
Eko ri konten sarve a tu.I RI happy meet A you I'm happy to meet you.
Kontenveni!happy-come Welcome!

Goodbye

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Tarsarve!after-meet See you later!
Tar!after Later!
Pertonire!sorry-go Bye!
Pertoneksire.sorry-out-go Goodbye.
Pona ire.good go Take care.

Well-wishing

A very general courtesy pattern is pona (good) plus whatever you want to wish goes or went well. It is an ordinary modifier on an ordinary noun, so you can build one on the spot for anything.

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Pona komi.good food Enjoy your meal.
Pona ire.good go Take care. / Safe travels.
Pona yu.good play Have fun.
Pona sana.good heal Get well.
Pona tormi.good sleep Sleep well.
Pona opus.good work Have a good day at work.

Pona plus the general time of day may be used both when greeting someone, and when parting.

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Pona suryan.good up-sun Good morning.
Pona yan.good sun Good day / Good afternoon.
Pona tunyan.good down-sun Good evening.
Pona nemyan.good no-sun Good night.

Reacting

Half of a conversation is showing that you are still in it. These are short on purpose; say them between the other person's sentences.

PikotikaMeaning
Si.Yes. / Right.
Ves, ker?Really?
Ves!Of course. / Exactly.
Sorpe!Wow!
Meka pona!That's great!
Eko ri vite.I see. (I understand)
Eko ri non sape.I don't know.
Eko ri konten oti a tis.I'm glad to hear it.
Eko ri perton oti a tis.I'm sorry to hear that.
Sammoto.Same here. / Likewise.
Meka yer, ker?Already? / So soon?

Encouraging someone

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Ponapos!good-if Good luck!
Tu ri kan vake a tis.you RI can do A this You can do it.
Tu ri nir.you RI near You're close.
Tu ri pona opus.you RI good work You're doing well.
Piko tar piko.small after small Little by little.
Pam repe tentar.please again try Please try again.
Non timo.not fear Don't worry.
Tis ri tiven mas nonsorin.this RI become more not-hard It gets easier.
Tentarimpar, kum tu ri vite.try-learn, and you RI understand Practice, and you'll get it.

Filling a pause

If you need a moment to think but do not want to give up your turn, fill the gap with e… — the vowel of English feather, held. It does the work of English um and ah, and it is close to what most languages already use, so nobody has to be taught it.

To buy a longer moment, say something:

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
Eko ri pensa.I RI think Let me think.
Pam sista piko.please stop small One moment.
Perton — eko ri non sape a tika.sorry — I RI not know A word Sorry — I don't know the word.
Pam repe tika, mas nonveros.please again say, more not-fast Say that again, more slowly.

Steering the conversation

Four words move it along. Each opens a sentence, and the rest of the sentence is ordinary.

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
nirtopinear-about speaking of which — a related thing
artopiother-about anyway, by the way — a new thing
invesin-true actually — correcting the impression
Pam oti:please hear Listen: — here comes the point

Listen — I'll be in your city next month.

Pam oti: 1 mese tar, eko ri in tu sitas.

For what goes between the greeting and the goodbye, see Small talk.

A whole conversation

Alice walks into someone on the street.

PikotikaEnglish
Aras: Perton! Eko ri non vite a tu.Alice: Sorry! I didn't see you.
Rokoomo: Nem marumkosa. Tu ri pona, ker?Local: No problem. Are you all right?
Aras: Si, mersi. Sets eko irevaso ri rompe vin.Alice: Yes, thanks. But my bag is broken.
Rokoomo: Eko ri kan servi a tu, ker?Local: Can I help you?
Aras: Meka mersi! Tis ri meka pesa.Alice: Thank you very much! It's very heavy.
Rokoomo: E… Pam sista piko. Si, tis ri pona in tistempo.Local: Um… One moment. There, that's better now.
Aras: Tu ri meka pona. Inves, eko ri veni in tisyan.Alice: You're very kind. Actually, I only arrived today.
Rokoomo: Kontenveni! Ponapos kum pona ire.Local: Welcome! Good luck, and take care.
Aras: Mersi. Pona tunyan!Alice: Thanks. Good evening!
Rokoomo: Pona tunyan. Tarsarve!Local: Good evening. See you around!
