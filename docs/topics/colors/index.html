Colors

Pikotika has six color words. Every other color is two of them put together, or something the color of a thing — so the whole system is six roots and one habit.

The six

  • rus red
  • yoro yellow
  • verte green
  • asur blue
  • anka white, pale
  • nero black, dark

The last two are not only colors. anka is anything light-valued and nero anything dark-valued, so they do the work English needs pale and dark for as well.

The word for color itself is koror — color, hue.

Mixing two

Join two color roots and you get the color between them.

  • rusyoro orange red-yellow
  • rusasur purple red-blue
  • asurverte cyan blue-green
  • rusanka pink red-white

The color of a thing

The rest are named after something that is that color, with koror as the head:

  • terakoror brown earth-color
  • risokoror tan, beige grain-color
  • rapiskoror grey stone-color

This pattern is open: any thing plus koror is a usable color word, and a listener who knows the thing knows the color.

Saying it

A color is a modifier, so it goes straight in front of what it colors — no verb, no agreement, no change of form.

What color is it?

Tis ri ker koror?

This is a yellow fruit.

Tis ri yoro pomo.

I want that big red ball.

Eko ri vori a nontis meka rus rotun.

Degree words work on colors as on any other modifier — meka nero is deep black, piko verte faintly green.

It's a tea shop that is green.

Tis ri verte arpoaku komparroko.

And these red ones — what are they?

Kum tis rus kosa ri ker?

Colors carry idioms too, and this one happens to be the same idiom as in English:

The boss gave us the green light.

Opustesta ri vertetika ver ekomen.
