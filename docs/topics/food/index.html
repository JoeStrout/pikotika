Food and eating

The root komi is both food and eating, and pipe is drink and drinking. These are the basis for a lot of food-related words, though there are some other relevant roots too.

General rules

A compound is head-final: the last root says what kind of thing it is, and the earlier roots narrow it down. Six roots do most of the heading.

HeadNamesExample
komia dish, a prepared food akukomi water-food soup
pipesomething you drink neropipe black-drink coffee
pomoa fruit or nut ruspomo red-fruit tomato
risoa grain or starch yororiso yellow-grain wheat
karnea meat aviskarne bird-meat chicken
akua thin liquid arpoaku plant-water tea

Length is a generality gradient, and food is where you see it most clearly. You can say as much as you need to be clear, given the context.

How it tastes

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning

saporkosataste-thinga spice
nerosaporkosablack-taste-thingpepper
wosaporkosahot-taste-thinga chili pepper
PikotikaMeaning
Tis ri meka ponasapor.This is delicious.
Tis ri wosapor, ker?Is it spicy?
Tis ri non tuke; tis ri piko nontuke.It isn't sweet; it's a little sour.
Eko ri kusta a nontuke.I like sour things.
Pam sapor a wun.Try one.
Komi ri nova.The food is fresh.

sapor is the taste itself, and as a verb it is tasting on purpose. tentar try, test works too, and carries a little more of ‘see what you think’.

Hungry, thirsty, full

Wanting is vori, and what you want goes in front of it; hunger and thirst are just wanting food or drink.

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
komivorifood-wanthungry
pipevoridrink-wantthirsty
venterpanstomach-allfull, had enough
PikotikaMeaning
Nino kum eko ri komivori.The child and I are hungry.
Eko ri mas pipevori.I'm thirsty again.
Eko ri venterpan. Tis ri meka komi.I'm full. That was a lot of food.
Eko ri komi vin.I've eaten.
Eko ri komi sista.I'm still eating.

Meals

A meal is named for the part of the day it falls in — morning, midday, evening with komi on the end.

PikotikaLiterallyMeaning
komitempofood-time a meal, mealtime
suryankomiup-sun-food breakfast
metseyankomimiddle-sun-food lunch
tunyankomidown-sun-food dinner, supper
pikokomismall-food a snack

Is breakfast included?

Suryankomi ri in moni, ker?

But come at seven, and we can eat first.

Sets pam veni in 7 ora, kum ekomen ri kan yer komi.

Ordering

Ordering is just wanting out loud: vori want or toma take, with what you want after a. (Remember that in Pikotika, it's not abrupt or rude to simply "want" something; you don't need to soften it as you would with words like "would like" in English.)

PikotikaMeaning
Tapur por 2 omo, pam.A table for two, please.
Eko ri kan toma a komiorten, ker?Could I have the menu?
Pits tu ri yervin, ker?Are you two ready to order?
Ker komi ri pona?What's good?
Tis ri ker?What is this?
Eko ri vori a akupeste, pam.I'll have the fish, please.
Kum 2 akuvaso aku, pam.And two glasses of water, please.
Eko ri vori a sam komi.I'll have the same.
Tu ri vori a arkosa, ker?Would you like anything else?
Nem arkosa, mersi.Nothing else, thank you.
Moniorten, pam.The check, please.

Note the difference between non and nem at a table: Non rikus, pam asks for it warm, while Nem rikus, pam asks for no ice. See Negation.

Please eat with utensils.

Pam komi in komiuter moto.

A utensil is a means, not a companion, so it takes in … moto and never kum — see Prepositional phrases.

What you don't eat

PikotikaMeaning
Eko ri non komi a karne.I don't eat meat.
Eko ri nonkarnekomiomo.I'm a vegetarian.
Tu ri tene a non karne komi, ker?Do you have anything without meat?
Karne ri in tis, ker?Is there meat in it?
Eko ri tene a sorinpomo korpononakor.I have a nut allergy.
Eko ri non kan komi a yororiso.I can't eat wheat.
Tu ri tene a nemyororisokomi, ker?Do you have gluten-free food?
Eko ri kan voritika ver komivakeomo a tis ri tiven non wosapor.I can ask the cook to make it not spicy.

korpononakor body-not-agree is an allergy — the body disagreeing with something. What you react to goes in front of it, as with any modifier.

A whole conversation

Lena and Mary are having dinner out.

PikotikaEnglish
Serviomo: Pona tunyan. Tapur por 2 omo, ker?Server: Good evening. A table for two?
Rena: Si, pam.Lena: Yes, please.
Serviomo: Tis ri komiorten. Pits tu ri yervin, ker?Server: Here's the menu. Are you two ready to order?
Meri: Nir. Tisnemyan, ker komi ri pona?Mary: Almost. What's good tonight?
Serviomo: Akupeste ri nova. Kum akukomi ri meka ponasapor.Server: The fish is fresh. And the soup is very good.
Rena: Eko ri non komi a akupeste. Tu ri tene a non karne komi, ker?Lena: I don't eat fish. Do you have anything without meat?
Serviomo: Si. Ekomen ri tene a ankariso komi kum vertekomi kum avisnino.Server: Yes — we have a rice dish with vegetables and eggs.
Rena: Eko ri vori a tis. Tis ri wosapor, ker?Lena: I'll have that. Is it spicy?
Serviomo: Piko. Eko ri kan voritika ver komivakeomo a tis ri tiven non wosapor.Server: A little. I can ask the cook to make it not spicy.
Rena: Non, piko ri pona.Lena: No, a little is fine.
Meri: Kum eko ri vori a akupeste, pam. Kum 2 akuvaso aku.Mary: And I'll have the fish, please. And two glasses of water.
Serviomo: Rikus sive wo, ker?Server: Cold or warm?
Meri: Rikus, pam.Mary: Cold, please.
Serviomo: Meka pona. Pona komi!Server: Very good. Enjoy your meal!
Tikakosaomo: Komitempo ri vin…Narrator: The meal is over…
Meri: Eko ri venterpan. Tis ri meka ponasapor.Mary: I'm full. That was delicious.
Rena: Ves! Tu ri vori a tukekomi, ker?Lena: It was! Do you want dessert?
Meri: Non, mersi. Sets eko ri vori a neropipe.Mary: No, thank you. But I'd like a coffee.
Rena: Serviomo, wun neropipe kum moniorten, pam.Lena: Server — one coffee and the check, please.
Meri: Eko ri kompar!Mary: It's on me!
Rena: Meka mersi.Lena: Thank you very much.
